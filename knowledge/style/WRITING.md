# Writing rules

Distilled from humanizer (MIT) and the unslop copy pass (CC BY 4.0); see [README.md](README.md).

## Say the thing

1. State the point. Drop "not X but Y" unless a reader really believes X.
2. No closing line that repeats the paragraph ("That is the real win").
3. No staged openers ("Here's the thing", "Let's dive in", "Quick note").
4. Do not argue with an objection nobody raised.
5. Threes only when there are three things.

## Plain words

6. No em or en dashes in prose. Use a period, comma, colon or parentheses.
   Code, commands, paths and URLs keep theirs.
7. Avoid the stock words: crucial, robust (figurative), seamless, delve, leverage, pivotal,
   showcase, tapestry, testament, vibrant, underscore (verb), meticulous.
8. No inflated significance ("a pivotal moment", "plays a key role") and no sales tone.
9. One hedge at most, and only when the doubt is real.
10. Name who acts. "The Sign job checks the certificate", not "the certificate is checked".

## Facts first

11. Never add a fact, number, date or name while rewording. Never drop one either.
12. Counts that change belong in generated files, not prose (AGENTS.md: numbers typed into
    prose are not facts).
13. Bold for a few key words, not for every list label. No emoji labels.
14. Credit lines stay exact: "Anthropic Claude models" and the owner's name.

## Scope

15. Living docs follow these rules. Dated records (`docs/review/`, `knowledge/archive/`) keep
    their original words; only their links may be repaired.
